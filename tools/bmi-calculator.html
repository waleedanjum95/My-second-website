What Is BMI?

Body Mass Index, commonly known as BMI, is a numerical measure calculated from a person's weight and height. It is commonly used as a screening tool to place adults into broad weight categories. SmartToolsKit's free BMI Calculator makes the calculation simple by allowing you to enter your height in centimeters and weight in kilograms.

BMI is calculated from height and weight rather than directly measuring body fat. Because of this, the result should be viewed as a screening measure rather than a complete assessment of an individual's health or body composition.

BMI Formula

BMI is calculated by dividing body weight in kilograms by height in meters squared.

BMI = Weight (kg) ÷ [Height (m) × Height (m)]

For example, a person weighing 70 kilograms with a height of 1.75 meters would have a BMI of approximately 22.9.

Standard Adult BMI Categories

For adults, commonly used BMI screening categories are based on the following ranges:

  • Underweight: BMI below 18.5
  • Healthy weight: BMI from 18.5 to 24.9
  • Overweight: BMI from 25.0 to 29.9
  • Obesity: BMI 30.0 or higher

Important Limitations of BMI

BMI does not directly measure body fat, muscle mass, bone density or the distribution of body fat. For example, people with a high amount of muscle may have a higher BMI without having a high level of body fat.

BMI categories can also be interpreted differently for children and teenagers because age and sex are important factors when assessing growth. Pregnancy is another situation where standard adult BMI interpretation is not appropriate.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is this BMI Calculator free?

What units does this BMI Calculator use?

What is a normal BMI for an adult?

A BMI from 18.5 to 24.9 is commonly classified as the healthy weight range for adults. BMI is a screening measure and should be interpreted in context.

Can BMI be used for children?

Standard adult BMI categories are not designed for children. Children's BMI is normally interpreted using age- and sex-specific growth references.

Does BMI directly measure body fat?

No. BMI is calculated from height and weight and does not directly measure body fat or body composition.
